simFastBOIN cheat sheet

Fast simulation of Bayesian optimal interval (BOIN) designs for phase I and phase I/II dose-finding trials: the maximum tolerated dose (MTD), the optimal biological dose (OBD) and combinations of two drugs, with the simulation engines written in C++.

simFastBOIN 2.3.0 Website Reference Introduction GitHub

1. Rules

Decision boundaries and tables for the protocol: boin_boundary(), boin_decision_table() and the tables of the other designs.

2. Scenarios

True DLT rates, and response rates for the OBD, with the true MTD and OBD: oc_scenarios().

3. Simulate

Operating characteristics of one scenario with sim_*(), of several with sim_*_multi().

4. Compare

Designs side by side and judged against the true doses: oc_results(), oc_compare() and plot().

5. Conduct

The next dose from the decision table or comb_boin_next(), the MTD or OBD at the end with the *_select_*() functions.

Every simulation function has an engine that returns the trials themselves, named after the design: boin_simulate(), tite_boin_simulate(), comb_boin_simulate(), boin_et_simulate() and so on. keep_trials = TRUE keeps them in the result of a sim_*() function.

Decision rules of the BOIN design

\(\lambda_e = \dfrac{\log\{(1-\phi_1)/(1-\phi)\}}{\log\{\phi(1-\phi_1)/[\phi_1(1-\phi)]\}}, \quad \lambda_d = \dfrac{\log\{(1-\phi)/(1-\phi_2)\}}{\log\{\phi_2(1-\phi)/[\phi(1-\phi_2)]\}}\)

\(\phi\) is target; \(\phi_1\) and \(\phi_2\) are p_saf and p_tox, \(0.6\phi\) and \(1.4\phi\) by default (boin_lambda()). With \(\hat p\) the observed DLT rate at the current dose:

Eescalate if \(\hat p \le \lambda_e\)
Sstay otherwise
Dde-escalate if \(\hat p \ge \lambda_d\)
DEde-escalate and eliminate this dose and above

A dose is eliminated when \(\Pr(p > \phi \mid \text{data}) > 0.95\) (cutoff_eli) under a uniform prior, from three patients on. At the end, the MTD is the dose whose isotonic estimate of the DLT rate is closest to \(\phi\).

library(simFastBOIN)

boin_boundary(target = 0.30, max_n = 18)

decisions <- boin_decision_table(target = 0.30, max_n = 12)
print(decisions, cohort_size = 3)
plot(decisions)                       # needs ggplot2

The result prints the true DLT rates, the selection and the average numbers of patients and DLTs at each dose, and the use of the doses above the target. With keep_trials = TRUE it also holds the trials. A seed gives the same trials as BOIN::get.oc() with the same arguments; set n_earlystop explicitly when comparing, since its default is 18 here and 100 in 'BOIN'.

Common arguments

targetTarget DLT rate; target_resp the response rate of the designs for the OBD, a target for BOIN-ET and the lowest acceptable rate for BOIN12 and BF-BOIN-ET
p_trueTrue DLT rates: a vector of the doses, or a matrix for two drugs, with the levels of drug A in the rows; p_resp the true response rates
n_cohort, cohort_sizeNumber of cohorts and patients per cohort
n_trials, seedNumber of simulated trials (10,000) and the seed (123)
start_doseDose of the first cohort (1, or c(1, 1) for two drugs)
n_earlystopStop once this many patients are at the current dose and the design would stay
cutoff_eliPosterior probability that eliminates a dose (0.95)
extrasafe, offsetStricter stopping rule at the lowest dose
titrationOne patient per dose until the first DLT; titration_doses ends it at a dose
window, accrual_rateAssessment window and patients per unit of time, for the designs with time
keep_trialsKeep the simulated trials in the result
boin_compatible, boinet_compatibleThe rules of 'BOIN' (waterfall) and of 'boinet' (BOIN-ET, TITE-BOIN-ET, gBOIN-ET and TITE-gBOIN-ET) where they differ from those of the package

The arguments of a design are those of its help page; the _multi() versions take scenarios in place of the true rates.

Prior information and toxicity grades

iBOIN moves the boundaries of every dose by a skeleton, a prior DLT rate for every dose, worth prior_ess patients.

skeleton <- c(0.10, 0.19, 0.30, 0.42, 0.54)
iboin_boundary(target = 0.30, skeleton = skeleton, prior_ess = 3,
               max_n = 9)
sim_iboin(target = 0.30, p_true = c(0.08, 0.15, 0.31, 0.45, 0.55),
          skeleton = skeleton, prior_ess = 3, n_cohort = 10,
          cohort_size = 3, n_earlystop = 100, n_trials = 2000, seed = 123)

robust = TRUE gives the robust prior; select_with_prior = FALSE selects the MTD from the data alone.

gBOIN decides from the toxicity grades, by the normalized score of each patient; the probabilities of the grades are a matrix with one row per grade and one column per dose.

p_grade <- rbind(
  "Grade 0-1" = c(0.83, 0.75, 0.62, 0.51, 0.34, 0.19),
  "Grade 2"   = c(0.12, 0.15, 0.18, 0.19, 0.16, 0.11),
  "Grade 3"   = c(0.04, 0.07, 0.11, 0.14, 0.15, 0.11),
  "Grade 4"   = c(0.01, 0.03, 0.09, 0.16, 0.35, 0.59)
)
sim_gboin(target = 0.47 / 1.5, p_grade = p_grade,
          score = c(0, 0.5, 1, 1.5), n_cohort = 10, cohort_size = 3,
          n_earlystop = 100, n_trials = 2000, seed = 123)

The target is on the scale of the score divided by its maximum: a target of 0.47 for scores up to 1.5 is 0.47 / 1.5. elim_rule = "count" eliminates doses with the rule of 'UnifiedDoseFinding'.

Late-onset toxicity and backfilling

TITE-BOIN treats new patients while earlier ones are still followed. The decisions also depend on the follow-up of the pending patients, by the single mean imputation (method = "imputation") or the effective sample size (method = "ess").

tite <- tite_boin_decision_table(target = 0.30, max_n = 15)
print(tite, cohort_size = 3)

sim_tite_boin(target = 0.30, p_true = c(0.05, 0.15, 0.30, 0.45, 0.60),
              n_cohort = 10, cohort_size = 3,
              window = 3,          # a three month assessment window
              accrual_rate = 2,    # two patients a month
              n_trials = 10000, seed = 123)

BF-BOIN backfills the patients who arrive while the escalation waits to lower doses that are safe and have shown a response; BE-BOIN does so around the time-to-event design, with the same arguments in sim_be_boin().

sim_bf_boin(target = 0.25, p_true = c(0.04, 0.12, 0.25, 0.43, 0.63),
            p_resp = c(0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.45, 0.58),
            n_cohort = 10, cohort_size = 3, window = 1,
            accrual_rate = 3, n_earlystop = 9, n_trials = 10000,
            seed = 123)

The results of the designs with time also give the duration of the trials.

The waterfall design finds the MTD contour, one combination at every level of drug A.

p_wf <- matrix(c(0.01, 0.04, 0.11, 0.15, 0.30,
                 0.03, 0.05, 0.13, 0.30, 0.50,
                 0.07, 0.10, 0.30, 0.48, 0.54), nrow = 3, byrow = TRUE)

boin_compatible = TRUE follows BOIN::get.oc.comb(mtd.contour = TRUE) trial by trial. Comb-BOIN12 and BOIN-ETC look for the optimal dose combination with p_resp as a matrix.

Optimal biological dose

The designs for the OBD also take the true response rates, p_resp, and select the dose that balances the response against the toxicity.

Compare designs and scenarios

oc_scenarios() gives the true MTD (and OBD) of every scenario, oc_results() sets the results of several designs side by side and oc_compare() judges them against the true doses. Nothing is simulated again.

scenarios <- list("Scenario 1" = c(0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.45),
                  "Scenario 2" = c(0.10, 0.30, 0.45, 0.55, 0.65))
truth <- oc_scenarios(scenarios, target = 0.30)

design <- list(target = 0.30, scenarios = scenarios, n_cohort = 10,
               cohort_size = 3, n_trials = 1000, seed = 123)
boin <- do.call(sim_boin_multi, design)
tite <- do.call(sim_tite_boin_multi,
                c(design, list(window = 1, accrual_rate = 2)))
three <- lapply(scenarios, function(p) oc_3p3(p_true = p))

results <- oc_results(BOIN = boin, "TITE-BOIN" = tite, "3+3" = three)
plot(results)                         # selection (%) by dose

comparison <- oc_compare(BOIN = boin, "TITE-BOIN" = tite, "3+3" = three,
                         truth = truth)
comparison
plot(comparison)

The designs must be all for one drug or all for two drugs, with the same doses. A design is given as the result of a sim_*() function, of its _multi() version, or as a list of results named by the scenarios. The waterfall design has its own summaries of the contour.

The 3+3 design

p_true <- c(0.05, 0.15, 0.25, 0.45, 0.60)
oc_3p3(p_true = p_true)                       # in closed form
oc_3p3(p_true = p_true, mtd_rule = "expand")
sim_3p3(p_true = p_true, n_trials = 10000)    # the same by simulation
decision_table_3p3()

mtd_rule = "previous" (the default) names the dose below the one declared too toxic; "expand" the highest dose with at most one DLT out of six patients.

Conduct a trial

The next dose is read off the decision table of the design (boin_decision_table(), tite_boin_decision_table(), boin_et_decision_table(), boin12_decision_table(), uboin_utility_table()), or given by comb_boin_next() for two drugs. At the end, the selection functions take the numbers of patients and DLTs of every dose, one row per trial.

n_pts <- matrix(c(3, 6, 9, 3), nrow = 1)
n_tox <- matrix(c(0, 1, 3, 2), nrow = 1)
boin_select_mtd(n_pts, n_tox, target = 0.30)
boin_isotonic(n_pts, n_tox)        # isotonic estimates of the DLT rates

How the package is checked

  • inst/reproduce/: scripts that reproduce the tables and figures of the articles of the designs.
  • inst/validation/: scripts that compare the package with 'BOIN', 'boinet', 'bfboinet', 'UnifiedDoseFinding', 'escalation' and the code of the authors; where the rules and the random numbers are the same, the results are equal.
  • Every compared value is PASS, EXPLAINED (a reason was found, listed in inst/validation/explained.csv), FAIL or INFO: How the package is checked.
  • inst/benchmark/benchmark.R times the package against the reference packages: Computation time.
